# Heat and Hot Water Obligations

Heat and hot water are essential services. Losing either is treated as an
immediately hazardous condition, and complaints about them always jump to the
top of the triage queue.

Heat season runs from October 1 through May 31. During heat season the
building must meet two temperature rules. Between 6:00 AM and 10:00 PM, if the
outdoor temperature falls below 55 degrees Fahrenheit, every apartment must be
heated to at least 68 degrees. Between 10:00 PM and 6:00 AM, apartments must be
kept at 62 degrees or warmer regardless of the outdoor temperature.

Hot water is a year-round obligation, not a seasonal one. Hot water must be
available at every tap and shower at a constant minimum of 120 degrees
Fahrenheit, every day of the year.

Triage guidance for the agent:

- "No heat" during heat season is an EMERGENCY-class condition. Confirm the
  unit, dispatch or escalate to an HVAC vendor the same day, and keep the
  tenant updated. Outside heat season, treat a broken heater as urgent but not
  an essential-service outage.
- "No hot water" is an EMERGENCY-class condition in any month. It usually
  points to the boiler or the unit's water heater and belongs with a plumber
  or heating contractor.
- Partial heat ("the bedroom radiator is cold but the living room is warm") is
  urgent rather than an emergency, but should still be scheduled within a day
  or two during winter.
- Space heaters are a stopgap, not a fix. If a tenant is relying on the oven
  or open flames for warmth, escalate immediately: that is a fire and carbon
  monoxide risk.

Failure to restore heat or hot water quickly exposes the owner to
emergency-repair charges, violations, and rent-withholding claims, so the
manager should always be told the same day the outage is reported.
