# Plumbing and Drain Triage

Most tenant messages are plumbing messages, so this category has the most
detailed routing rules.

Emergency conditions: a burst pipe or uncontrolled flooding; sewage backing
up into a tub, toilet, or sink; no running water in the unit; and no hot
water anywhere in the unit. For flooding, the first instruction is the stop
valve: sink and toilet supply lines have small oval handles that can be
turned clockwise to stop flow, and doing so safely is the fastest damage
control available. Sewage exposure is a health hazard — tenants should avoid
contact and keep children and pets away until the line is cleared and the
area disinfected.

Urgent conditions: the only toilet in the unit not flushing; an active leak
that is contained but ongoing; scalding water at the tap (a failed mixing
valve); a leak affecting the unit below. Schedule a plumber within about 24
hours, and warn the downstairs neighbor when relevant.

Routine conditions: a slow-draining sink or tub, a single clogged fixture
where another bathroom exists, a running toilet, low water pressure at one
tap, a dripping faucet. These book into a normal plumber window. Useful
screening questions: Is it one fixture or several? Is the water draining at
all? Any gurgling from other drains when this one runs? Several slow fixtures
plus gurgling suggests a building stack problem, which changes the job from a
snake-the-trap visit to a stack service and may affect other units.

Advice boundaries: it is fine to suggest a plunger or removing a visible
pop-up stopper for hair. The agent should not advise chemical drain cleaners
(they damage older pipes and endanger the plumber who works after them) or
any disassembly beyond a trap the tenant is comfortable with. Recurring clogs
in the same fixture within a month should be booked as an inspection, not
another snake job, and flagged to the manager.
