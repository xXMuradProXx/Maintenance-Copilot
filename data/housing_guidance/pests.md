# Pest Management Obligations

Owners of multiple-dwelling buildings are required to keep apartments free of
pest infestations and to use integrated pest management rather than one-off
spraying. In practice that means sealing entry holes and cracks, fixing the
moisture sources pests need, removing nesting conditions, and only then
applying targeted treatment by a licensed exterminator.

Rodents. Mice and rats are hazardous conditions; an active rodent infestation
is treated as immediately hazardous. Schedule a licensed exterminator quickly,
and pair the visit with a handyman task list: droppings along a wall usually
mean a gap behind a stove, radiator, or pipe penetration that must be sealed
or the problem will return.

Roaches and other insects. A roach infestation is a hazardous condition that
should be scheduled promptly, normally within a few days. Ask the tenant which
rooms are affected and whether neighbors report the same problem — insects in
several units mean the building line must be treated together, which changes
the vendor booking from one visit to several.

Bed bugs. Bed bug complaints require a careful, documented response: an
inspection of the reporting unit and commonly the adjacent units (either
side, above, and below), preparation instructions for the tenant, and usually
more than one treatment visit spaced about two weeks apart. Owners must also
keep records of bed bug history, so every complaint and treatment should be
logged in the case file.

Communication guidance: tenants often feel embarrassed reporting pests. The
agent should make clear that infestations are a building responsibility, not
a judgment about housekeeping, ask for photos only if the tenant is
comfortable providing them, and explain preparation steps (clearing cabinets,
bagging linens) well before the exterminator's visit.
