# Work-Order Triage Playbook

This playbook defines how a raw tenant message becomes a work order the
manager can trust.

Step 1 — extract. From the tenant's own words, capture: the unit (never
guess it), the affected room or fixture, what is happening, since when,
whether it is getting worse, and any safety signal (water near electricity,
smells, sparks, children or elderly residents affected). Vague phrases map to
concrete hypotheses: "water near the light" means possible ceiling leak over
a fixture — an electrical risk, not just a plumbing annoyance; "bathroom
problem" means ask one clarifying question rather than guessing.

Step 2 — classify. Match the message to a taxonomy category and problem
code. Prefer the more severe candidate when two codes are plausible and the
message is ambiguous; it is cheaper to downgrade after a question than to
miss an emergency.

Step 3 — prioritize. EMERGENCY means essential services (heat, hot water,
water, gas, power), active flooding, sewage, fire/gas/CO signals, unsecured
entry doors, or structural collapse signs. URGENT means hazardous but
contained: leaks, mold, pests, the only toilet, broken locks or glass.
ROUTINE is everything cosmetic or convenience-level.

Step 4 — decide the single next state. Every triage turn ends in exactly one
of: question sent to tenant (missing facts), windows offered (awaiting tenant
choice), appointment booked (tenant chose), escalated (manager judgment
needed), or resolved (tenant confirmed fixed). Cases must never be left in
limbo without an owner.

Escalate to the human manager whenever: the safety pre-filter fired;
classification confidence is low; the tenant mentions a lawyer, rent
withholding, or a housing complaint; the tenant is elderly, disabled, or
mentions children affected by the condition; the same issue has come back
within 30 days; or the cost or vendor needed is outside the approved list.
Escalation is a feature, not a failure: the record travels with the case so
the manager starts with full context.
