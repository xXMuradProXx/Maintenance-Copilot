# Electrical Hazards

Electrical complaints are triaged by one question: is there any sign of heat,
sparking, or exposed conductors? If yes, the condition is immediately
hazardous.

Emergency-level electrical conditions include sparking outlets or switches, a
burning or "fishy" plastic smell near any device or panel, scorch marks,
outlets or cords that are hot to the touch, buzzing from the panel, exposed
or hanging wires, and any electrical fixture in contact with water. For these,
tell the tenant to stop using the fixture immediately and, if it is safe and
they know how, to switch off the relevant breaker. Never advise a tenant to
open a panel, splice wires, or work on anything energized. A licensed
electrician must be dispatched the same day and the manager alerted; if there
is smoke or fire the tenant must leave and call 911 first.

Urgent but not emergency: a whole apartment without power while neighbors
have power (often a tripped main or a failed breaker), a breaker that trips
repeatedly, or a bathroom or kitchen GFCI outlet that will not reset. These
should get an electrician within about 24 hours. Repeated tripping is a
warning sign of an overloaded or failing circuit and should never be "solved"
by taping the breaker on.

Routine: a single dead outlet with no burning smell, a flickering or dead
light fixture, a broken switch plate. Schedule these at a normal window, but
ask the screening questions first — "does the outlet look discolored, feel
warm, or smell?" — because tenant wording like "the electric thing sparks
when I turn it on" describes an emergency even when the tone sounds casual.

All electrical repair work beyond changing a bulb or a cover plate is
licensed-trade work. Route these jobs to the electrician vendor category,
never to a general handyman.
