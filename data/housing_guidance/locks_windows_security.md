# Locks, Doors, Windows, and Security

Security conditions are triaged by whether the unit can currently be secured.

Break-ins. If a tenant reports a break-in or an apartment door that has been
forced or kicked, safety comes first: if there is any chance an intruder is
present, the tenant should not enter and should call the police. Once the
scene is safe, an un-securable entry door is an emergency work order — a
locksmith or carpenter is dispatched the same day, with a temporary board-up
or hasp acceptable overnight if a full repair must wait. The manager is
alerted immediately, and the police report number is recorded in the case.

Broken entry locks. An apartment entrance door that cannot lock is a
hazardous condition even without a break-in, and should be repaired within a
day. Tenants are entitled to a functioning lock, and where permitted may
request a lock change at their own arrangement — but management must retain
lawful access per the lease, so unauthorized lock swaps get flagged to the
manager rather than argued over chat.

Lockouts. A tenant locked out of their apartment is urgent for the tenant
but is a service call, not a hazard. Verify identity against the tenant
record before dispatching a locksmith or authorizing a spare key, and note
that lockout charges may apply per the lease.

Windows. Broken window glass is urgent: it is a laceration hazard and a
security gap on low floors and fire escapes. A window that will not close is
urgent in cold weather and routine otherwise. Window guards deserve special
care: where a child ten years old or younger lives in the unit, approved
window guards are required on openable windows, guards may never be removed
or left broken, and a missing or broken guard report is treated as
immediately hazardous. Stuck-window jobs must never involve removing a guard
without a compliant replacement going straight back in.
